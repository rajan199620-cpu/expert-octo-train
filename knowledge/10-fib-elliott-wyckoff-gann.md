# 10 · Fibonacci, Elliott Wave, Wyckoff, Gann

These frameworks are **interpretive**. Two skilled practitioners can read the same
chart differently. They are included because many market participants use them,
which can make the levels they produce self-fulfilling. They should **never** be your only reason
for a trade.

## Fibonacci

**Retracements** of a completed swing: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50% (not a Fibonacci ratio, but
traditional), 61.8% (the "golden ratio"), 78.6%.
- Shallow pullbacks (23.6–38.2%) mean a strong trend. 50–61.8% is the typical "value zone". A pullback beyond 78.6% usually means the trend has failed.
- **Extensions** for targets: 127.2%, 161.8%, 261.8% of the prior leg.
- **Confluence** is what matters. A Fibonacci level matters when it coincides with prior S/R, an MA or a gap.
- **Critiques:** the choice of swing is subjective, and with five levels some level is always "respected". No convincing evidence shows that Fibonacci ratios beat arbitrary ratios.
- **Engine:** reports retracements of the last zigzag swing. The roaster always reminds you of the above.

## Elliott Wave Theory (R.N. Elliott, 1938; Frost & Prechter)

- Trends unfold in **5 waves** (1-2-3-4-5: impulse) and corrections in **3 waves** (A-B-C), in fractal fashion.
- **Hard rules:** wave 2 never retraces more than 100% of wave 1. Wave 3 is never the shortest impulse wave. Wave 4 doesn't overlap wave 1's price territory (except in diagonals).
- **Guidelines:** wave 3 is often 1.618× wave 1. Wave 2 often retraces 50–61.8%, and wave 4 38.2%. Alternation (if wave 2 is sharp, wave 4 is sideways).
- **Critique:** extremely flexible. Counts get revised after the fact, so the theory is hard to falsify in real time. The hard rules are useful for **invalidation**: if wave 4 overlaps wave 1, the count is wrong.

## Wyckoff Method (Richard Wyckoff, 1930s)

**Three laws:** (1) supply and demand drive price; (2) cause and effect (the size of a
trading range, the "cause", predicts the size of the following move, the "effect"); (3) effort vs result
(volume vs price progress).

**Composite Man:** imagine a single large operator who accumulates quietly, marks price up,
distributes, then marks it down.

**Accumulation schematic (phases A–E):**
- **A:** PS (preliminary support), SC (selling climax: huge volume), AR (automatic rally), ST (secondary test).
- **B:** building the cause; tests within the range.
- **C:** **Spring**, a dip below support that quickly reverses (a shakeout) and is tested on low volume.
- **D:** SOS (sign of strength), LPS (last point of support), a move toward the top of the range.
- **E:** markup out of the range.

**Distribution** is the mirror: PSY, BC (buying climax), AR, ST, **UTAD** (upthrust after
distribution, a false breakout above resistance), SOW (sign of weakness), LPSY, markdown.

Wyckoff is the most useful of the four because it is built on **volume and structure**,
which can be checked.

## W.D. Gann

Angles (1×1 = one unit of price per unit of time), squares of price and time, and seasonal and
cyclical dates. It is highly esoteric, with little rigorous evidence, and widely mythologised. It is included
for completeness. **Treat it as folklore unless you can test it.**

## Failure modes

- Unfalsifiability: wave counts and Fibonacci levels can be fitted to anything after the fact.
- Confirmation bias: you remember the time 61.8% held, and forget the four times it didn't.
- Using these instead of risk management rather than alongside it.
