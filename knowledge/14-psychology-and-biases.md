# 14 · Psychology & biases

TA is partly applied crowd psychology, and partly a minefield of the analyst's own
psychology. The roaster exists mainly because of this chapter.

## Why crowds create patterns

| Bias | Market footprint |
|---|---|
| **Anchoring** | Prior highs and lows and purchase prices become S/R ("I'll sell when I get back to break-even") |
| **Disposition effect** (Shefrin & Statman) | People sell winners too early and hold losers too long, which creates overhead supply and makes trends drift |
| **Herding** | Trends extend beyond fundamentals; breakouts attract followers |
| **Under-reaction / conservatism** | Slow adjustment to news leads to momentum and post-earnings drift |
| **Over-reaction** | Climaxes, then mean reversion |
| **Recency** | Extrapolating the last few bars; sentiment extremes |
| **Loss aversion** (Kahneman & Tversky) | Losses hurt about 2× as much as equal gains feel good, so people panic at the lows |

## Biases that wreck the analyst

| Bias | What it looks like | Antidote |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation bias** | Only noticing the indicators that agree with you | Write the bear case before buying (the roaster does this) |
| **Apophenia / pattern-seeking** | Seeing H&S patterns in noise | Require confirmation; use objective detection |
| **Hindsight bias** | "It was obvious" | Journal your predictions *before* the outcome |
| **Overconfidence** | Oversizing after a win streak | Fixed-fractional sizing |
| **Sunk-cost fallacy** | "I'm down too much to sell" | The market doesn't know your entry price |
| **Outcome bias** | Judging a decision by its result | Judge the process: did you follow the plan? |
| **Narrative fallacy** | A story makes the chart feel certain | Ask what price would prove the story wrong |
| **FOMO** | Chasing extended moves | Extension check (ATR distance from MA) |
| **Revenge trading** | Doubling up after a loss | Daily loss limit |
| **Authority bias** | Trading Telegram/YouTube tips | Ask: if they knew, why tell you? |

## Process disciplines

1. **Pre-commit:** entry, stop, target and size are written down *before* the order.
2. **Journal:** setup, reason, screenshot, emotion, result in R, lesson.
3. **Review in batches of 20+ trades.** One trade is noise.
4. **Separate analysis from execution.** Decide when the market is closed.
5. **Have a "no trade" option.** Most days, it's the right one.
