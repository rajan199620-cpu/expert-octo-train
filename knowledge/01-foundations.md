# 01 · Foundations

## What technical analysis is

Technical analysis studies market **price and volume** (and things derived from them)
to judge the odds of where price goes next and, more importantly, **where a trade
idea is proven wrong**. Fundamental analysis asks *what* to own. Technical
analysis mostly asks *when* and *how much*.

## The three classic premises (Charles Dow, later formalised by Murphy)

1. **Price discounts everything.** All known information (earnings, macro, sentiment)
   is reflected in price. *Critique:* only true to the extent markets are efficient.
   If they were fully efficient, TA would not work at all.
2. **Prices move in trends.** A trend in motion is more likely to continue than to
   reverse. This is the claim with the strongest empirical support, through the
   time-series momentum literature (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012).
3. **History rhymes.** Crowd psychology repeats, so recurring chart patterns reflect
   recurring human behaviour (fear, greed, anchoring, herding).

## Dow Theory (the six tenets)

1. **The market has three movements.** The *primary* trend lasts months to years. *Secondary* reactions retrace 1/3–2/3 of the primary move over weeks to months. *Minor* swings last days to weeks and are mostly noise.
2. **Primary trends have three phases.**
   - Bull: *accumulation* (informed buying while news is bad) → *public participation* (trend followers join; the biggest move) → *excess/distribution* (euphoria, retail piles in, smart money sells).
   - Bear: *distribution* → *panic* → *despair*.
3. **The market discounts all news.**
4. **Averages must confirm each other.** Originally the Industrials and the Rails. Today you might compare large caps with small caps, or a stock with its sector.
5. **Volume must confirm the trend.** Volume should expand in the direction of the primary trend.
6. **A trend stays in force until it gives a definite reversal signal.** An uptrend is a series of higher highs and higher lows. It ends only when that sequence breaks.

## Market cycle / stage model

Stan Weinstein's four stages (based on the 30-week MA, often used as a 40-week/200-day MA):

| Stage | Name | Price vs 30-wk MA | MA slope | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Basing | Oscillating around | Flat | Watch; don't buy yet |
| 2 | Advancing | Above | Rising | Buy breakouts and pullbacks |
| 3 | Topping | Oscillating around | Flattening | Tighten stops; take profits |
| 4 | Declining | Below | Falling | Avoid longs or short |

Wyckoff's cycle (accumulation → markup → distribution → markdown) is the same idea
viewed through supply and demand. See chapter 10.

## Timeframes

| Style | Chart | Holding period | Primary tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| Position / investing | Weekly/monthly | Months–years | 40-wk MA, stages, relative strength |
| Swing | Daily | Days–weeks | Structure, 20/50 MA, RSI, patterns |
| Day trading | 1–60 min | Intraday | VWAP, volume profile, opening range |

**Rule:** use one timeframe higher than the one you trade to set the trend, and
one lower to time entries (chapter 12).

## Why TA might work (theoretical justifications)

- **Behavioural finance:** under-reaction to news (anchoring, conservatism) creates trends. Over-reaction then produces reversals (Barberis, Shleifer & Vishny 1998; Hong & Stein 1999).
- **Self-fulfilling behaviour:** many participants watch the same levels (round numbers, 200-day MA, prior highs). Orders cluster there.
- **Institutional flow:** large funds take days to weeks to build positions, which leaves footprints in volume and persistent drift.
- **Adaptive Markets Hypothesis (Lo 2004):** edges appear and decay as participants adapt, so a rule can work in one era and stop in the next.

## Why it might not (the efficient-market objection)

- The **weak-form EMH** says past prices cannot predict future returns once costs are counted.
- Many apparent edges disappear after **transaction costs** and **data-snooping corrections** (chapter 15).
- Pattern recognition is subjective, and humans see patterns in random data. Charts of random walks "show" head-and-shoulders patterns too.

## Failure modes

- Treating the premises as facts rather than hypotheses.
- Ignoring that one well-known edge (e.g. a 200-day MA cross) is watched by everyone and may be arbitraged away.
- Using a timeframe that doesn't match your holding period.
