## 1. What UPSC prelims actually asks (web research)

- **Format:** In 2026, 67 of 100 GS-1 questions were multi-statement and 10 were match-the-pair. Fewer than 15 were direct factual recall. Elimination across two or three plausible statements is now the core skill.
- **How current affairs is tested:** About a third of the paper was linked to current affairs, but rarely as standalone facts. Only about 5 questions were pure "current affairs/schemes". The news item is the hook and the static concept is what gets tested. One analysis estimates a pure newspaper reader could answer about 29 questions in 2015 but only about 13 in 2025.
- **Weightage (2025–26):** Economy about 15–20, Polity 11–14, Environment 9–13, Geography about 13, S&T up to 15 (a jump in 2026: AI/LLMs, drones, quantum). Modern History is about 8; Art & Culture is small.
- **Recurring prelims categories:** constitutional articles, Acts and rules, SC judgments, schemes, reports and indices, institutions, places in news, species and protected areas, international bodies, and key terms.

## 3. Errors found in the paper itself (don't learn these from TH)

- **p18:** "67 nuclear tests in the Marshall Islands **in 1954**". The 67 tests ran from 1946 to 1958; 1954 is the year of Castle Bravo.
- **p16:** Musahars are called a "tribal community". In UP and Bihar they are a notified **Scheduled Caste**.
- **p11:** Rajendra Prasad is called "India's first President" on 24 Jan 1950. On that date he spoke as **President of the Constituent Assembly**.

## 4. Roast of this analysis

**Red Team.** The cards mostly train single-fact recall (cloze). The exam mostly tests multi-statement elimination. A card saying "s.31 RPA 1950" will not prepare you for a statement like "Only an ERO may file a Form 7 objection" being false. About 16 of the 88 cards are low-yield numbers or trivia (GST share, PMI value, Coal India targets, drug-sample counts, Iraq's deadline, Asian Games, NIMHANS, the Monegar Choultry). UPSC almost never asks exact numbers.

**First-principles auditor.** Known: what the paper printed. Assumed: (a) the paper is accurate, and section 3 shows it isn't always; (b) the [static] facts I attached are correct. I checked two of them online (VB-G RAM G, the Mahanadi tribunal chair). The rest come from my own knowledge, and the "20% agricultural drought threshold" is the editorial's framing, not verified. (c) The October 2026 news is inside the 2027 prelims window, which is true for a May/June 2027 exam.

**Operator.** 88 cards from one day works out to about 2,600 a month. Your review load would bury the static syllabus within weeks. A sustainable rate is roughly 15 to 25 new current-affairs cards a day.

**Skeptical counterparty (the paper-setter).** The examiner won't ask "import GST share = 32.2%". They might ask "IGST on imports is levied by the Centre" or "PMI above 50 indicates…". They would happily use the Form 7/RPA cluster, Art. 171, the triple test, Art. 262, khadar vs bangar, Kaliningrad, the Dikhow, VB-G RAM G and the tribal-art mapping.

**Steelman.** About 45 cards are exactly the hook-plus-static pattern the exam uses. The polity/electoral cluster alone covers half a dozen likely statement themes. The [static] labelling and the "paper error" notes also protect you from learning wrong facts.

## 5. Import notes

- Anki **tags cannot contain spaces**. "current affairs October 2026" would split into three tags, so the patch uses `current_affairs_October_2026`. Every card also carries a subject tag `CA_Oct26::<Subject>`; you can delete it in the import dialog's tag field if you don't want it.
- The file is a TSV with a `#notetype column` header, which needs Anki 2.1.55 or later. It contains Basic and Cloze notes. Import via File → Import and choose your deck there; the file sets no deck.
