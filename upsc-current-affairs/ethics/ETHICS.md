- Ethics is **GS Paper IV (Mains)**, not prelims. Section A (theory, about 125 marks) and Section B (6 case studies, about 125 marks).
- **2025 paper:**
  - Section A: 6 questions on ethics and integrity, 3 on attitude and emotional intelligence, 4 philosophical. 5 questions named Indian or global thinkers directly.
  - Section B: case studies drawn from real life: disasters, environmental clearances, welfare corruption, procurement, conflict of interest, refugees and border security.
- **Recurring themes:**
  - digital and social-media ethics (2019, 2021, 2025)
  - constitutional morality (2017, 2020, 2025)
  - governance and public-administration dilemmas
  - growth vs justice, environment vs security
- **What scores:** apt, specific examples tied to the value being asked. Toppers say personal and administrative examples beat generic or historical ones. The standard bank includes Ashok Khemka, Satyendra Dubey and Singapore's CPIB.

## 2. Roast of "do this just for ethics"

**Red Team.** Most GS-IV marks come from structure, stakeholder analysis and judgment, especially in the case studies. Anki can't train any of that. A daily paper is also mostly *allegations*: vote-theft claims, party feuds, pending cases. Citing contested, partisan material as fact in an ethics answer is a real way to lose marks. Most of today's ethics-flavoured news fails that filter. The most likely failure is a growing pile of politically loaded "examples" you can't safely use.

**First-Principles Auditor.**
- **Known:** Section A rewards a fitting example for a named value (courage, probity, conflict of interest, empathy…), and you have to retrieve it under time pressure. About 20 questions in 3 hours is roughly 9 minutes each.
- **The load-bearing assumption:** retrieval is the bottleneck. That holds for Section A. It does not hold for case studies.
- **Second assumption:** news examples stay usable for 6–12 months. That's true only if the example illustrates a value and doesn't hinge on a disputed fact.

**Operator.** This paper yielded about 8 usable themes out of 22 pages. A daily stream would add roughly 150 notes a month, which breaks the no-bloat rule. It only works as a **fixed-size bank**: one note per syllabus theme (about 40 in total), with the theme as the first field. A better example replaces the old one on re-import ("update existing notes when first field matches"), so the deck never grows. Cost: about 2 minutes a day to judge whether anything beats what the bank already holds.

**Skeptical Counterparty (the examiner).** They don't care that a pilot was stabbed. They care whether you tie it precisely to "courage = acting despite fear, duty over self". A bare news fact is decoration. Each card therefore needs a one-line **"Use:"**, the value it proves. The examiner also rewards thinkers and quotes; this paper gave one good line (Narsinh Mehta, *peed parai jaane re*).

**Steelman.** Example retrieval is the single GS-IV sub-skill where spaced repetition clearly helps. Forty notes reviewed in about 3 minutes a day can enrich 10–12 Section A answers, and 2026 examples beat the overused Khemka and Dubey. The format also protects you: neutral framing, flagged pending cases, a source on every card.
